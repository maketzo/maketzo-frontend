IDENTITY

The grief of becoming boring enough to profit

The trader who actually makes money looks nothing like the one you pictured becoming. Nobody warns you how much of yourself you have to kill to get there.

Jeremy B. · Mon Oct 19

A trader I know makes money every month. He is the most boring person I have ever met.

Same two setups. Same window. Same size. In by mid morning, flat before lunch, offline before the afternoon noise starts. He doesn't watch the close. He doesn't scan after hours. If you ask what he did today, he says the usual. If you ask what he did last Tuesday, same answer. Last March, same.

You don't want to be him. That is the entire problem.

WHAT YOU SIGNED UP FOR

Nobody opens an account to become a civil servant

You came for the opposite of boring. The variance. The heat. The feeling of being inside something unpredictable and coming out the other side. The identity of being a trader, which is a word that carries a specific energy in your head and that energy is not methodical office worker who clocks out at 1pm.

Nobody buys a charting platform at 9pm on a Sunday because they dream of a smaller life. You bought it because you wanted the bigger one. More control over your time, sure, but also more intensity per hour of that time. More signal. More consequence.

So you built a schedule around action. You scanned every gapper. You took the second entry when the first missed. You watched the full session because what if the move came at 2:40. You chased the news. You traded through lunch because flat was unproductive. You were, by any measure, engaged.

None of that is what makes money.

THE INCOMPATIBILITY

Two different people are competing for the same seat

The personality that is drawn to trading and the personality that pays the bills from trading are not the same person. They barely share a kitchen. One wants stimulation and the other wants a checklist. One wants to be inside the move and the other wants to be in cash by 11:30 having taken one A+ setup and nothing else.

The personality that wants to trade is incompatible with the personality that makes money trading. Nobody tells you that going in because nobody would sign up.

Every honest profitable small-cap trader I've met in six years looks, from the outside, like they're doing less than you. Fewer trades. Fewer tickers. Fewer screens. Fewer opinions. They are not more exciting than you. They are visibly, aggressively less exciting than you. That is not a stylistic choice. That is the job.

THE GRIEF

What you actually have to kill

The gap between you and him is not education. You know the setups. You know what size you should be in. You know that revenge trading is a loop and that the make-back trade is how one red day becomes a red month. You know all of it.

The gap is grief. You have to grieve a version of yourself that was supposed to be here by now. The one who read like a monk, pressed size, called tops, traveled on the P&L. You have to walk past that guy in the hallway every morning and keep going to the small desk where the small routine lives.

Specifically, you have to grieve:

  • The three A+ looking setups you'll pass on today because they aren't in the two you've chosen to be good at.
  • The afternoon you won't watch, even on the days the morning was flat and your brain is still looking for a trade to justify the chair.
  • The ticker everyone in your chat is making money on that you have no business being in.
  • The size up after a green week, which is dopamine wearing a strategy costume.
  • The identity of being someone with an opinion on the day. The boring version doesn't have one.

Each of these is a small funeral. There are a lot of them. The grief isn't dramatic, which is part of why most traders don't recognize it as grief. It just feels like something is missing from the day. Something is. The exciting version of you is missing. On purpose.

THE SHAPE OF THE BORING ONE

He's not better. He's smaller.

The boring trader is not more intelligent than you. He is not reading the tape with some gift. He has made a smaller version of himself and refuses to leave it. His edge is not pattern recognition. His edge is refusal.

He refuses the third trade. He refuses the size he could technically justify. He refuses to look at the afternoon. He refuses to have a thesis on something he hasn't traded fifty times. He refuses to let a green open convert into a green-to-red by staying one trade too long. His whole career is a stack of refusals and it looks, to an outsider, like he isn't doing much.

He isn't. That is the point.

If you watched him for a week you'd think his process was underwhelming. If you ran it for a year, you'd be a different person at the end. That is the trade. Not money for risk. Self for consistency.

THE HANDOFF

The grief is the part you can outsource

Here is the honest thing. The reason most traders can't become the boring version of themselves is not that they don't understand the math. It's that the grief is lonely. You are sitting at a desk alone, voluntarily subtracting things from your own day, and nobody is clapping. Your Discord isn't clapping. Your P&L on a good disciplined day looks identical to a mediocre one.

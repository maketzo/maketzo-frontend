RISK

Every size-up hires a new trader with no track record

The green trade quietly promotes a bigger version of you into a job she has never rehearsed, and the market is paying attention.

Kelly M. · Tue Nov 17

The trader who got you to green is not the trader you promoted. You rewarded one and sent a different one back into the ring. Nobody tells you that. The brokerage doesn't flag it. Your P&L doesn't flag it. The only thing that flags it, eventually, is the next week.

Four green days at 500 shares. On day five, you take 1,500 on the cleanest setup of the morning. You call that confidence. It is not confidence. It is a promotion you handed to someone with no resume.

THE PROMOTION

You hired a new trader and forgot to tell her.

The 500-share trader had a sample. Call it sixty reps, call it a hundred, call it a year of knowing exactly how a gap-and-go behaves when you have 500 shares in it. She knew what a half-position reduce felt like with her fingers. She knew what her hands wanted to do when the first candle after entry printed red. She had scars in exactly the right places.

The 1,500-share trader has none of that. She has your login, your account balance, and a chair that is still warm from somebody else. Her first trade is not trade one hundred and one. It is trade one.

EDGES DON'T SCALE

Your edge has a share count attached to it.

This is the part nobody writes on the whiteboard. An edge is not a chart pattern. It is a chart pattern plus a share count plus a nervous system that has rehearsed what that specific share count does to your decisions. Change the share count and you have changed two of the three.

Your edge was built at a size. It does not get in the car with you when you move up.

At 500 shares, an eight-cent adverse move is forty dollars and a shrug. At 1,500, the same eight cents is one hundred and twenty and a flinch. The flinch moves your hand. The hand clicks out early, or worse, freezes. Your average loss widens. Your slippage profile changes because you are now taking two prints to fill instead of one. The time between entry and panic shortens. None of this is on the chart. All of it is in the next month's P&L.

You are not trading a bigger version of the same trade. You are trading a different trade with the same ticker.

THE DRIFT

Nobody decides to size up. They drift.

You do not sit down on a Monday and write in your journal, "I am now a 1,500 share trader." If you did, you would at least have to argue with yourself. The drift is quieter than that.

You take one trade at 750 because the setup was really clean. You take the next at 1,000 because the first one worked. By Thursday afternoon, a 500-share clip feels small and almost embarrassing to send. By the following Monday, the hot keys have been reprogrammed to a new default and you cannot remember when you did it.

The decision was never made at a desk with coffee. It was made on a Wednesday afternoon when a chart was clean enough that your hand reached for fifty percent more size, and nobody was in the room to ask why. Drift is a decision made by nobody, which is what makes it so hard to undo.

THE ANSWER

Every size-up is a new account with no track record.

Here is the sentence to tape above your hot keys. Every size-up resets the sample to zero. Your 4R week at 500 shares tells you nothing, literally nothing, about what your P&L looks like at 1,500. It feels like it does. It does not. You are starting a new backtest, with real money, in live conditions, on a nervous system that has not been tested at the new amount.

Every size-up opens a new account with no track record and the market's full attention.

The first ten trades at the new clip are expensive data. If they go well, you get to keep trading at the new clip. If they go badly, which is statistically more likely than you want to believe because you have no sample and you are now the rookie in a bigger weight class, you give back the streak that bought you the promotion and then some. That is not bad luck. That is the predictable cost of trading a size you had no evidence worked.

The green trade sets up the red one because the green trade quietly hires a new trader, writes her a bigger check, and sends her out to prove herself on a size nobody rehearsed. She is going to lose. The math says so. The green streak was never the problem. The silent promotion was.

WHAT HOLDS

Size is a decision for a cold brain, not a hot one.

You cannot solve this with willpower during the trade. The hot brain does not negotiate well with the cold brain, and it certainly does not take size instructions from it mid-click. The clip has to be decided before the market opens, by the version of you who has no position on and no reason to lie.

That is the one job of a Morning Lock-In. You write the max share count for the day while you are calm, and that number binds the version of you who is three green trades in and starting to feel large. The lock-in is boring on a good day and load-bearing on the day you almost promoted the wrong trader.

TRADERVUE REVIEW

Is TraderVue Worth It for a Small Cap Day Trader?

TraderVue is worth it if your problem is not knowing your numbers: it pulls in trades from more than 80 platforms and turns them into charts and reports that show exactly where the money goes. It is the wrong tool if your problem is breaking rules you already know, because everything it does happens after the trade, and a small cap trader's costliest decisions get made in the minute after a loss.

Kelly M. · Wed Nov 11

The Report That Found the Leak Seven Trades Late

Picture a small cap trader on a Tuesday. At 9:52 AM a gapper fails at its premarket high, and the trader cuts it for a loss at the planned price. Ninety seconds later the same stock dips again, and the trader buys it back to win the money back. It fails again. A third entry follows at 10:05.

On Wednesday the urge returns after the first loss, and this time the re-entry works. The stock rips 20 cents the right way and the loss is gone in four minutes. On Thursday come two more re-entries, both losers.

Sunday night every trade is in TraderVue, imported and tagged. The filter for re-entries after a loss shows seven trades. Six lost, and together they cost more than the rest of the week made. The time-of-day report shows the damage bunched within five minutes of a prior loss.

TraderVue did its job perfectly. It found the leak, priced it to the cent and dated every drop. It found it seven trades after it started, and after the habit had been paid once.

Lay that week next to any tool you are considering and ask one question: when does this tool first see the mistake? The answer tells you what it can fix.

What TraderVue Does Well

Start with what earns TraderVue its place. The strengths are real:

  • Imports. TraderVue lists "80+ Trading Platform Integrations," DAS Trader among them, across stocks, options, futures and forex. If you trade through more than one broker, it can keep everything in one place.
  • Charts on every trade. In its own words, you "review your trades with automatically generated price charts on multiple timeframes, from weekly down to 1-minute." Seeing your entry on the 1-minute chart a week later is humbling and useful.
  • Tags and filters. Tag every setup and every mistake, then filter any report by tag. That is how the re-entry leak got found at all.
  • Reports. Results by time of day, day of week, price range, setup and trade duration. Gold adds exit analysis, which shows what your exits left on the table.
  • Mentors and sharing. Paid plans allow unlimited mentors, and you can share trades with the community for feedback.

Here is the rule of thumb: if you cannot say which setup and which half hour pay you, a month of TraderVue imports will tell you. For a trader who has never measured anything, that alone can be worth the fee.

How Much Does TraderVue Cost per Month?

TraderVue publishes three tiers:

  • Free. "Users on the free plan have a monthly import limit of 30 trades," in the words of TraderVue's help center. The count is grouped trades, not executions, and it runs by the month you import them.
  • Silver, $29.95 a month. Unlimited trade imports, broker sync and advanced reporting.
  • Gold, $49.95 a month. Everything in Silver plus trade exit performance analysis, max potential P&L analysis and support for commissions and fees.

Paid trials take a card and convert automatically unless you switch to the free plan before they end.

Count your round trips before you pick. A small cap trader who takes five round trips a session fills the free plan's 30 trades in six trading days, so for most active traders the real choice is Silver or Gold. If exit analysis is the report you want most, budget for Gold.

Is there a free alternative to TraderVue? Its own free tier is one, for a slow month. A spreadsheet is another, and it reports just as late.

What TraderVue Misses for a Small Cap Trader

The gaps are not bugs. They come from what TraderVue is: a review tool.

  • It only sees finished trades. TraderVue describes itself as a way to "track and review every trade, no matter how old." Nothing in its own description of the product acts while you trade, and it has no place to write a rule and check yourself against it.
  • It grades results. Every report is built from P&L, win rate and the trades themselves. A rule you broke on a winning trade shows up as a winning trade, unless you remembered to tag it, and remembering is the very habit that fails on a bad morning.
  • It arrives on your schedule. The weekly review is when you choose to sit down. The re-entry on Wednesday paid inside four minutes. A reward that lands in minutes beats a correction that lands days later, nearly every time.
  • The best analysis costs the most. Exit analysis and commissions sit on Gold, and an active trader outgrows the free plan in about a week.

None of this is unique to TraderVue's users. Professionals do the Tuesday re-entry too. A study of proprietary traders at the Chicago Board of Trade found they "appear highly loss-averse, regularly assuming above-average afternoon risk to recover from morning losses."[1] Those were full-time traders with every report they wanted, and the pull to win it back was still there in the afternoon.

If you stay on TraderVue, add the review it cannot do for you: one written line after every exit, before you open another chart, naming the rule the trade followed or broke. That one line reaches the minute the reports never will.

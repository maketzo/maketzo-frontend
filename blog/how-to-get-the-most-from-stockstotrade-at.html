How to Get the Most From StocksToTrade at the Open

You get the most from StocksToTrade at the open by trading it the way its lead trainer, Tim Bohen, teaches: let the Oracle scanner find the day's names, let the open settle, and take only the few that fit your plan. The scanner hands you 20 trading opportunities at the opening bell, and the most valuable rule in the subscription costs nothing: wait until about 9:45.

Jeremy B. · Sat Nov 14

How Much Does StocksToTrade Cost?

Know what you are buying before the bell, because the platform does a lot more than one scanner.

StocksToTrade's pricing page lists two plans: $179.95 a month, or $1,899.50 for a year, which the page calls a $250 saving. Both include the software and StocksToTrade University, its library of training videos. Extras such as the Breaking News Chat room, where its analysts post breaking news in real time, and Level 2 can be added when you subscribe. Spread across a month of about 21 sessions, the monthly plan comes to roughly $8.60 a day.

The platform puts a news streamer, each company's SEC filings and news, pre-programmed scans built on Tim Sykes's strategies and paper trading for practice in one place. Sykes and Zak Westphal co-founded StocksToTrade in 2012, Westphal runs it as CEO, and Bohen, its lead trainer, hosts its Premarket Prep and writes much of its teaching on discipline.

His teaching is the playbook for the open, and the rest of this morning runs on it.

9:30: Let Oracle Do the Finding

The bell rings, the Oracle list fills, and the slowest part of a small cap morning is already done.

StocksToTrade describes Oracle in one line: it "sifts through 15,000 stocks every second, searching for 5-to-1 risk ratios, generating 20 trading opportunities right at the opening bell." Each name arrives with suggested signals and support and resistance levels. Building a list like that by hand would eat the whole open.

Treat the twenty as a shortlist to cut, not a list to work through. Bohen's number for new traders is "zero to three times a day." Twenty names to choose from and three trades at most is a strong place to stand: the scanner gives you options, so you can afford to be picky.

Being picky is a skill, and you can train it before it counts. MAKETZO's Focus Room opens with the Warm-Up, eight short games that train the decisions trading asks for, among them waiting for the edge, cutting what is not working and passing on a fake breakout. Five minutes of it before the bell is practice for exactly the choices the Oracle list will put in front of you, so passing on the first fakeout of the day is a move you have already made that morning.

9:31 to 9:45: Run Tim Bohen's 9:45 Rule

The most valuable thing in a StocksToTrade subscription costs nothing: wait until about 9:45.

Bohen spells the rule out in Waiting Can Be the Best Strategy: "it's a window between something like 9:40 and 10:00, as long as you're not trading at 9:30. The point is to wait until after the market opens and the dust settles before jumping in." By then the twenty names have shown you which ones held their move after the bell and which ones faded. The same article puts the whole idea in one sentence: "Sometimes, the best trade is no trade at all."

Fifteen minutes is a long time to sit still with twenty names moving. MAKETZO's Waiting Room is built for exactly that stretch: it gives you something to do while you wait, so the wait does not turn restless and Bohen's rule survives the first minute of the open.

9:45: Give Every Name Two Reasons Beyond the Alert

An Oracle hit tells you where to look. Taking the trade needs two more reasons.

Bohen sets the bar plainly: "A random trade with one flimsy reason, like it's 'up big,' isn't good enough." His answer fits on a sticky note: "Plan your trade. Prepare for it. Stick to it." So before a name moves from the list to an order, say two things out loud: what the news is, and where you are wrong. If both come easily, the name has earned a look at your plan. If not, it goes back on the list, and the list still has plenty in it.

He also names the sign that a morning has slipped away from the plan, in his piece on trading burnout: "Your plan calls for 3 to 5 trades and you're taking 30." The cause, in the next breath: "You're trading to be doing something, not because you see quality setups." MAKETZO's COACH SAYS is built for that moment. It is short, direct coaching that steps in the moment your behavior starts drifting from the rules you set, so Bohen's warning reaches you at the trade, not at the end of the day.

After the Close: Track Your Data the Way Bohen Asks

Bohen's advice for new traders comes with homework: "track your data and refine your strategy."

Two numbers tell you whether your mornings are working: how many trades you took, and your cents per share. A month where the count stays near your plan and the cents per share climb is a month the platform paid for itself.

A 9 or 10 means you ran the day clean, and when Bohen's playbook becomes your habit, the score shows it week by week instead of leaving you to guess.

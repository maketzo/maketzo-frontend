9. Instrument-specific risk warnings

Different financial instruments carry different and often substantial risks:

  • Stocks (equities). Subject to issuer risk, market risk, liquidity risk, gap risk, and the risk of total loss in the case of bankruptcy or delisting. Small-cap and low-float securities can move violently on low volume and may be subject to short-squeeze, pump-and-dump, or halts.
  • Options. Options can expire worthless. Buyers can lose 100% of their premium. Writers (sellers) of options can incur losses substantially greater than the premium received, including unlimited losses on uncovered short calls. Read the OCC's Characteristics and Risks of Standardized Options before trading.
  • Futures. Futures are leveraged. Small price moves can produce gains or losses that far exceed the margin posted. Losses can exceed your initial deposit.
  • Foreign exchange (forex). The high leverage commonly available in forex trading can work for or against you. Forex carries country, sovereign, and counterparty risk in addition to market risk.
  • Cryptocurrencies and digital assets. Volatile, often unregulated or partially regulated, subject to custody risk, exchange failure risk, smart-contract risk, regulatory risk, and the risk of total loss. Digital assets are not deposits and are typically not insured by FDIC, SIPC, or any government agency.
  • Leveraged and inverse ETFs. Designed to track daily returns; over longer holding periods, returns can diverge significantly from the underlying due to compounding and decay.

MAKETZO is currently focused on small-cap day-trading discipline. References to other instruments are educational. Trading any instrument other than the one for which you have explicitly developed a plan, risk-management framework, and account suitability is at your sole risk.

10. Margin, leverage, and the Pattern Day Trader rule

Trading on margin amplifies both gains and losses. You can lose more than the funds you deposit. Margin calls can require you to deposit additional funds or close positions on short notice. Your broker may liquidate positions without notifying you in advance to satisfy a margin call.

In the United States, FINRA Rule 4210 (the "Pattern Day Trader" or PDT rule) requires that any margin customer who executes four or more day trades in a rolling five-business-day period in a margin account maintain a minimum equity of $25,000 in that account. Failure to maintain this minimum results in restrictions or a margin call. We do not monitor your account for PDT compliance. That is your responsibility and your broker's responsibility.

Leverage caps and margin requirements vary by jurisdiction, by instrument, and by broker. You are responsible for understanding the rules of your account and your jurisdiction before placing any trade.
